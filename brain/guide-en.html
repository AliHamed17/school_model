What you will build

  1. A picture of 5×5 squares (pixels). A dark square is 1, an empty square is 0.
  2. A “brain” made of 25 weights (one per square) and a bias. The brain adds up weight × pixel and gets a score.
  3. After every picture the brain checks its answer and nudges the weights. That nudging is learning.
  4. At the end the brain is tested on pictures it has never seen. Goal: 90% or more.

Before you start

  1. Excel on a computer (Excel for the web also works).
  2. Download the Starter file at the top of this page and open it.
  3. Yellow cells = you type. Blue cells = formulas (you type them, or they are already there).

0The idea in one minute▶ Watch this part 0:07

An AI does not “see” an apple. It sees numbers. Our brain gives every square a weight. Squares that appear in apples get a positive weight. Squares that appear in bananas get a negative weight.

Score = bias + (weight 1 × pixel 1) + (weight 2 × pixel 2) + … + (weight 25 × pixel 25). A big score means Apple, a small score means Banana. The probability is 1/(1+EXP(-score)): a score of 0 is 50%.

Real AIs like Teachable Machine do exactly this — with millions of weights instead of 25.

3Teach the brain: type 5 formulas▶ Watch this part 0:52

Open the Learn sheet. Row 7 is the starting brain (all zeros). In row 8 the brain looks at picture 1. Click each cell and type (or click a formula to copy it):

Tip: click a formula below to copy it, then paste it into the cell.

CellFormulaMeaning
F8=L7+SUMPRODUCT(M8:AK8,AL7:BJ7)bias + sum of weight × pixel
G8=1/(1+EXP(-F8))probability that it is an Apple
K8=E8-G8answer − probability (how wrong)
L8=L7+$E$3*K8new bias = old bias + learning rate × error
AL8=AL7+$E$3*$K8*M8new weight 1 = old weight + learning rate × error × pixel
  1. Type the formula in F8, G8, K8 and L8. Press Enter after each.
  2. Type the weight formula in AL8. Then select AL8, grab the fill handle and drag right to BJ8 (25 weights).
  3. Now select F8 and drag across to BJ199… or easier: click F8, hold Shift, click BJ199, then press Ctrl+D (Fill Down).
Look at column I (Mistake?): 1 = the brain was wrong on this picture. Early rows have mistakes. Later rows have none: the brain has learned.
✔ Column F shows numbers down to row 199 and the Brain sheet is no longer empty.

4Watch it learn▶ Watch this part 2:17

  1. Open the Brain sheet. The two charts show mistakes in each round and loss (how wrong the brain is). Both should go down.
  2. Change the learning rate (yellow cell C4) to 0.05, then 0.5, then 5. Look at the charts each time.
  3. Which learning rate reaches 0 mistakes first? Which one is slowest?
Too small = slow learning. Too big = jumpy. This is the most important setting in real AI training.

6Test the brain▶ Watch this part 2:52

Open the Test sheet. The 10 pictures are new. Type these formulas in row 4 and fill down to row 13:

CellFormulaMeaning
C4='Learn'!$L$199+SUMPRODUCT('Data'!D22:AB22,'Learn'!$AL$199:$BJ$199)score of test picture 1
D4=1/(1+EXP(-C4))probability
E4=IF(D4>=0.5,"Apple","Banana")the brain's answer
F4=IF(B4=E4,1,0)1 if it is right
C16=COUNT(F4:F13)how many pictures
C17=SUM(F4:F13)how many right
C18=C17/C16accuracy (format as %)
  1. Type the formulas in C4, D4, E4 and F4. Select C4:F4, drag the fill handle down to row 13.
  2. In C16, C17 and C18 type the three summary formulas. In C18 press % on the Home tab.
  3. Look at the pictures that are wrong (red in column F). What do they have in common?
With the 12 training pictures the brain gets about 7 of 10 (70%). Not enough for the goal of 90%.

Questions

The Brain sheet says “fill the Learn sheet first”.

You have not typed the formulas on the Learn sheet yet (or not filled them down to row 199).

I see #NAME? or #REF!.

A sheet name in a formula is wrong. Click the cell on the sheet instead of typing the sheet name.

My numbers differ a little from the guide.

If you changed a picture or the learning rate, the numbers change. That is correct.

Can I use Google Sheets?

Yes for the formulas, but the colours and charts may look different. Excel is recommended.

This brain is a simple model (logistic regression). Teachable Machine uses a bigger model, but the idea of weights that are nudged is the same.
